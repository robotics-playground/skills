Pixel calibration

The defect tolerance comes from the image, not a guess. Edge sharpness is compared with a simulated perfect edge sampled the same way.

Smoothing may move an edge at most 0.105 mm, the precision limit for reading an edge from one anti-aliased pixel. Anything larger, such as the notch between the two fists or the thumb-base corners, is real drawing and is kept. Rebuilt corner tips are the one exception: they move up to one effective pixel (0.27 mm here) to restore the sharp vertex the image blurred.

How it was built

The method follows the research on image-to-CAD: understand the object first, use the image for exact edges, and check every round with numbers and an overlay.

  1. Understand the object

    A flat three-colour 3D-printed plate in a block base. Eleven parts named, with design rules and the front-to-back order read from seams on your photo.

  2. Rank the sources

    Artwork for shape and design intent, the print photos for real construction, your measurements for scale. Conflicts went to you; you chose the artwork.

  3. Fit a simple shape per part

    Ellipses, rounded boxes and strokes decide which part each region belongs to and complete hidden edges. Where the real print shows a clean geometric shape, the shape is used exactly: the arms are one oval ring (an outer oval minus an inner oval), the jacket is a straight-sided panel, and the cravat is a straight-line V.

  4. Trace visible edges, remove only pixel defects

    Edge pixels are unmixed into the two paints they blend, compression specks are dropped, and curves are smoothed within a tolerance measured from the image itself (below). Corners that the image's softness rounded are rebuilt sharp where both sides are straight.

  5. Check every round

    Overlap scores per part, largest shift in mm, gap and sliver checks between parts, minimum printable thickness, and the overlay above.

  6. Report, don't change

    Features that conflict with printing are flagged for you to decide, never altered.
